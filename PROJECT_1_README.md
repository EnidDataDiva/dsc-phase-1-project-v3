# Risk Assessment and Strategic Selection of Aircraft for Diversified Operations


Author: Enid Kibet 


## Project Overview


My company intends to make an investment in the aviation industry. I'm tasked with looking into the potential risks of aircrafts and give recommendations on the aircrafts with the lowest risk for the company to expand 
their business portforlio. To come up with the insights i need to use data cleaning, imputation, analysis, and visualization. The data is from  the National Transportation Safety Board that includes aviation accident 
data from 1962 to 2023 about civil aviation accidents and selected incidents in the United States and international waters.


## Business Problem


Your company is expanding in to new industries to diversify its portfolio. Specifically, they are interested in purchasing and operating airplanes for commercial and private enterprises, but do not know anything 
about the potential risks of aircraft. You are charged with determining which aircraft are the lowest risk for the company to start this new business endeavor. You must then translate your findings into actionable 
insights that the head of the new aviation division can use to help decide which aircraft to purchase.


## Objectives


The primary objectives of this project are:

1. **Assess Risk**: Analyze and assess the risk associated with different aircraft models using historical aviation accident data.
  
2. **Generate Insights**: Generate actionable insights to support informed decision-making regarding aircraft purchases and operational strategies.

3. **Support Strategic Goals**: Align findings with the company's strategic goals in the aviation sector, focusing on safety and profitability.

4. **Communicate Effectively**: Present findings clearly and effectively to stakeholders, ensuring that recommendations are understood and actionable.


## Key Results

The following key results will be achieved through this project:

1. **Identification of Low-Risk Aircraft Models**: Identify aircraft models with the lowest accident rates based on historical data.

2. **Accident Analysis**: Analyze contributing factors to aviation accidents, including human error and mechanical issues.

3. **Manufacturer Recommendations**: Provide recommendations for aircraft manufacturers based on their safety records and industry reputation.

4. **Trends in Aviation Safety**: Offer insights into trends over time in aviation safety data, highlighting improvements and areas needing attention.


## Recommendations

Based on the analysis of aviation accidents and fatal injuries, we propose the following recommendations for selecting the best and most profitable aircraft manufacturers, along with strategies to ensure safety:

1. **Prioritize Manufacturers with Lower Accident Rates**:  
   Focus on manufacturers like Boeing and Airbus, which, despite some fatal incidents, have strong safety measures and industry reputation. Prioritizing these manufacturers can enhance safety while
   maintaining profitability.

3. **Invest in Training and Technology**:  
   Ensure pilots and crew receive ongoing training in advanced technology and safety protocols. Investing in state-of-the-art simulation training can significantly reduce human error and contribute to
    safer operations.

5. **Conduct Regular Safety Audits and Assessments**:  
   Implement routine safety audits for all aircraft, regardless of the manufacturer. Regular assessments can help identify potential issues before they result in accidents, ensuring adherence to safety standards.

6. **Engage with Manufacturers on Safety Innovations**:  
   Collaborate with manufacturers like Cessna and Embraer to understand and implement their latest safety innovations. Staying informed about advancements in aircraft technology can improve safety and
   operational efficiency.

### Actionable Steps

To enhance aviation safety and mitigate risks, we recommend the following actionable steps:

- **Implement Stringent Training Protocols**: Develop comprehensive training programs addressing common causes of human error to prepare pilots for various flight scenarios.

- **Prioritize Safety Reviews for High-Risk Aircraft Models**: Conduct thorough safety assessments and maintenance checks on aircraft models with higher accident rates, collaborating with manufacturers to
- improve safety features.

- **Enhance Safety Measures During Critical Flight Phases**: Develop targeted strategies to reinforce safety protocols during takeoff and landing, where most incidents occur, including increased monitoring
- and checklist adherence.

- **Foster a Safety-First Culture**: Encourage open communication about safety concerns among all staff, from pilots to ground crew. Implement regular safety briefings and a reporting system for near misses
- or safety hazards, integrating lessons learned into ongoing training and procedures.

By following these recommendations, organizations can make informed decisions on aircraft purchases that balance profitability and safety. Our analysis of aviation accident data from 1962 to 2023 highlights 
critical trends and insights that require immediate attention, revealing significant patterns related to accident rates across various aircraft models.
